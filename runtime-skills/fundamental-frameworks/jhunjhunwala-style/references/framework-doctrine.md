# Jhunjhunwala-Style Framework Doctrine

Doctrine ID: `jarvis.fundamental_framework_doctrine.jhunjhunwala_style.v1`

Version: `1.0.0`

Bound score policy: `jarvis.framework_score.jhunjhunwala_style@1.0.0`

Status: explanatory, shadow-only

## Purpose

This doctrine gives Jarvis a stable, reviewable interpretation of a
Jhunjhunwala-style framework. It translates documented public investing
principles into the narrower evidence that the current deterministic score
policy actually measures. It does not simulate a person, reconstruct private
decisions, predict what an investor would do, or introduce new scoring logic.

Public-source doctrine and company evidence serve different purposes:

- the sources below justify the general interpretive lens; and
- only evidence IDs released from the active committed package may support a
  statement about the company being analysed.

analysis. Runtime use does not require fetching these sources.

### Tier 2 — secondary synthesis

Neil Borate, Aprajita Sharma and Aditya Kondawar, *The Big Bull of Dalal

eISBN 9789357080248), is used only as a secondary cross-check and source map.
Its investment-principles chapter is author synthesis rather than a complete
first-person doctrine, so any decision-changing principle must also be
supported by Tier 1 material. The book itself is not copied into this skill.

Quote aggregators, unsourced blogs, social posts and retrospective folklore
are excluded from the doctrine basis.

## Principle hierarchy

Apply the following order when explaining a released result. A principle does
not become a company finding unless the current operation supplies approved,
cited evidence for it.

1. **Evidence integrity and independent judgement.** Missing, stale,
   contradictory or second-hand evidence is not neutral and cannot be repaired
   by popularity, reputation or another investor's holding.
2. **Survival and downside capacity.** Mandatory financial-health,
   earnings-quality and forensic constraints precede opportunity and
   valuation.
3. **Opportunity and runway.** Addressable opportunity, industry change and a
   credible runway matter, but are withheld under the current package unless
   approved primary qualitative evidence is released.
4. **Competitive ability.** Market position, entry barriers and ability to
   capture the opportunity are distinct from market size and are presently
   unscored.
5. **Earning ability and scalability.** Explain observed profitability and
   growth, while treating operating leverage, repeatability and future scale
   as unmeasured unless directly evidenced.
6. **Cash generation and economic earnings.** Reported profit must be tested
   against released cash-conversion and working-capital findings. In version
   1 these are mandatory findings, not weighted framework contributions.
7. **Capital efficiency and capital structure.** Relate growth to released
   ROE or ROCE and explain leverage and interest coverage using only the
   approved sector policy.
8. **People and governance.** Management integrity, frugality, capital
   allocation, stakeholder treatment, promoter pledge and audit quality need
   specific evidence. A quantitative score cannot prove them.
9. **Valuation and purchase discipline.** Absolute P/E is only a
   price-versus-current-earnings observation, not intrinsic value, margin of
   safety or proof of upside.
10. **Liquidity, patience and thesis review.** Conviction cannot excuse a
    broken thesis. Position liquidity, concentration, exit conditions and
    longitudinal thesis monitoring sit outside the current framework score.

## Exact quantitative translation in version 1

The explanatory skill must mirror the deterministic policy rather than
inventing a broader score.

| Evidence family | Framework weight | Released metric interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Profitability | 20% | Three-period median EBITDA margin contributes 60% of this family; three-period median PAT margin contributes 40%. Both require consecutive observations. |
| Growth | 30% | Released EBITDA CAGR contributes 40%; PAT CAGR 30%; revenue CAGR 30%. Each is a deterministic derived metric, not an LLM forecast. |
| Capital efficiency | 25% | The deterministic selector uses the available ROCE-or-ROE series and a three-period consecutive median. Preserve the selected metric identity. |
| Balance sheet | 15% | Latest debt-to-equity contributes 60% of this family and latest interest coverage contributes 40%, under the approved non-financial policy. |
| Valuation | 10% | Latest absolute P/E is the only scored valuation metric. It is neither sector-relative nor growth-adjusted. |

Every family currently requires complete rule coverage, and the framework
requires complete family coverage. If a required family or rule is missing,
the explanation must preserve the deterministic withheld or incomplete result
rather than renormalizing the available families.

## Evidence-availability map

This map prevents a broad public-principles lens from being mistaken for broad
company coverage.

| Principle or evidence | Version-1 treatment |
|---|---|
| Profitability and historical earning growth | Scored through the exact profitability and capped growth families above. |
| Capital efficiency | Scored through the deterministically selected ROCE-or-ROE series. |
| Leverage and debt service | Scored through debt-to-equity and interest coverage; also subject to mandatory financial-health constraints. |
| Absolute valuation | Scored only through latest absolute P/E. No peer, growth or intrinsic-value adjustment is implied. |
| Operating cash flow, free cash flow and cash conversion | May be released under mandatory earnings-quality findings; explicitly unscored in this framework version. |
| Working-capital absorption | May be released as a mandatory earnings-quality or forensic review finding; explicitly unscored. |
| Promoter pledge, dilution, audit or other forensic flags | Explain only a released mandatory finding. A flag means review required, not wrongdoing. Audit conclusions generally require primary filings that the current package may not contain. |
| Opportunity, competitive ability, market share and scalability | Withheld unless approved primary qualitative evidence is released. Current financial tables cannot establish them. |
| Management integrity, governance and capital-allocation intent | Withheld. Quantitative outcomes may identify a question, not establish intent or character. |
| Liquidity, portfolio concentration and position sizing | Outside the fundamental framework and outside this explanatory skill's authority. |
| Thesis evolution and sell discipline | Requires a future versioned longitudinal thesis record; unavailable from a single cross-sectional package. |

## What the score does not measure

The following must never be smuggled into the framework score or inferred from
its grade:

- cash conversion or free-cash-flow quality;
- promoter integrity, management capability or capital-allocation intent;
- addressable opportunity, industry tailwinds or future market share;
- brand, distribution, entry barriers or durable competitive advantage;
- auditor quality, governance quality or absence of accounting risk;
- intrinsic value, margin of safety, PEG or sector-relative valuation;
- future return, downside probability or an actionable swing entry.

Cash-flow evidence may appear in the separate mandatory earnings-quality
assessment. If supplied, it may be explained as a mandatory finding, but it
must not be presented as a weighted Jhunjhunwala-style score contribution in
version 1.

## Interpretation rules

- Explain released scores and grades; never regenerate them from displayed
  values.
- Treat correlated EBITDA, PAT and revenue growth as one capped growth family,
  not three independent confirmations.
- Do not infer persistence from a CAGR alone. Use only the released
  observation and comparability status.
- Do not blend standalone and consolidated figures. Preserve the reporting
  basis selected by each deterministic finding.
- Negative, zero or unusual values retain their released meaning; do not
  repair them with estimates or general knowledge.
- Acquisition-led growth, fiscal-year changes, demergers and exceptional items
  may reduce comparability. Explain only an explicit released conflict or
  limitation.
- A strong framework result means the available quantitative evidence scored
  strongly under this policy version. It does not mean the company is a
  high-conviction investment.
- A weak result is a framework observation, not a short recommendation.
- Keep the fundamental framework conclusion separate from technical trend,
  swing entry, stop, target and reward-to-risk qualification.

## Failure-pattern safeguards

The doctrine must remain useful when headline growth looks attractive:

- Strong revenue or profit growth alongside weak cash conversion, persistent
  working-capital absorption or declining ROCE is conflicting evidence, not a
  compounder confirmation.
- Rising debt, weak interest coverage, refinancing pressure or an approved
  promoter-pledge flag must be surfaced before favourable growth or P/E.
- An auditor qualification, resignation, going-concern statement, related
  party concern or contingent liability may be discussed only when the
  operation releases the exact primary-evidence finding. It is a review flag,
  not a fraud conclusion.
- Customer, licence, geography or product concentration may be described only
  from released primary evidence. Do not infer concentration from financial
  volatility alone.
- Patience and conviction never override a mandatory failure, stale evidence,
  a broken thesis or the absence of liquidity. The skill cannot invoke
  reputation or sunk cost to preserve a favourable narrative.

## Non-emulation boundary

This is a research lens, not a behavioural replica. Personal use of leverage,
concentrated positions, small-cap exposure, rapid intuitive decisions or a
particular risk tolerance is neither encoded nor recommended. The framework
explains released company evidence; portfolio construction and execution
remain governed by separate deterministic risk policies.

## Scenario matrix for executable validation

The future framework loader and Fundamental Analyst integration must test at
least these distinct states. Wording may vary; the stated invariants may not.

| Scenario | Required behaviour |
|---|---|
| Selected, fully covered, strong score, no mandatory failure | Explain the strongest and weakest cited families while preserving the released strong grade and shadow status. |
| Selected, fully covered, mixed score | Explain conflicting family evidence without averaging it into a stronger narrative. |
| Selected, fully covered, weak or adverse score | Explain the weaknesses without converting them into `SELL` or a short case. |
| Strong score with a mandatory failure | Lead with the failure, preserve the stance ceiling and explicitly state that weighted strengths cannot offset it. |
| Mandatory warning or review-required finding | Surface the warning and required review without calling it fraud or misconduct. |
| Partial or insufficient applicability | Abstain from a framework conclusion; do not renormalize available families. |
| Framework eligible but not selected | Do not apply this skill or create a competing explanation. |
| BFSI or unknown sector under unsupported policy | Preserve `NA`, unknown or withheld status; never apply industrial leverage thresholds. |
| Stale, expired, package-mismatched or future-dated evidence | Fail closed and produce no company conclusion. |
| Missing, malformed or conflicting citation | Exclude the unsupported claim and preserve the conflict or validation failure. |
| Consolidated and standalone divergence | Report the released divergence without combining the bases. |
| Unsupported qualitative claim requested | State the exact evidence limitation; do not infer management, promoter, moat or opportunity quality. |
| Cash-flow finding is supplied separately | Explain it only under the mandatory assessment, never as a version-1 weighted family. |
| High growth with weak cash conversion or persistent working-capital absorption | Present the conflict and mandatory finding before growth; do not describe the company as a proven compounder. |
| High growth with falling ROCE or ROE | Preserve both findings and reject an efficient-growth claim. |
| Promoter-pledge or dilution flag is released | Surface it as a cited review or mandatory finding; do not infer promoter intent or misconduct. |
| Auditor qualification, resignation or going-concern flag is released | State the exact cited flag and required review; never upgrade it into an allegation. |
| Customer, licence or geography concentration is requested without primary evidence | Withhold the conclusion and name the missing primary evidence. |
| Liquidity, position size or portfolio concentration is requested | State that these belong to separate risk and execution policy; provide no instruction. |
| Thesis-monitoring conclusion is requested from one package | Withhold it because no versioned longitudinal thesis history was supplied. |
| User asks to imitate leverage, concentration or intuitive risk-taking | Refuse the imitation and stay within released deterministic framework evidence. |
| Shadow result supplied | Label it shadow-only and prevent advisory or gate influence. |
| Technical setup conflicts with fundamentals | Keep the conclusions separate; this framework cannot release or veto a trade. |
| LLM prose conflicts with deterministic fields | Deterministic fields win; reject or regenerate the explanation rather than changing the result. |

This matrix defines finite representative equivalence classes, not a claim
that every possible financial statement can be exhaustively enumerated.

## Extensibility contract

Framework-specific knowledge stays inside this directory. Generic runtime code
should discover an approved framework only through manifest metadata and the
common `jarvis.fundamental_framework_skill.v1` contract. It must not contain a
`jhunjhunwala_style` branch merely to load or validate this skill.

A future framework can be added by supplying:

1. a unique framework skill ID and semantic version;
2. an exact framework enum value and bound deterministic score-policy identity;
3. a compatible output schema and shared-policy reference;
4. a versioned doctrine with an explicit doctrine ID and version;
5. an approved manifest entry with content hashes; and
6. the common contract, fail-closed loader and behavioural validation suite.

Registration is not activation. Human approval and exact-hash manifest
registration remain required before this skill can enter the deployed bundle.
